    How the rig works

    One skeleton for five birds

    Pip, Kiwi, Mango, Kakadu and Rojo will share these bones and pivots. Each bird is its own part sheet and palette, so every pose and move is written once and works for all of them.

    Parts, not frames

    A pose is a list of bone angles plus which drawing each slot shows. Eyes, beaks and wing shapes swap; everything else bends and turns. New poses cost a few numbers, not new art.

    A head that turns

    Face parts ride an invisible sphere. Turn Pip and the near eye grows, the beak leads, the lower body twists the other way and the tail swings to the far side.

    Alive between moves

    Pip breathes, blinks, glances around, and its crest and tail lag behind a hop and settle on springs. Its beak moves with its voice. All of it stops for people who ask for reduced motion.

    Built for the app

    One small script with no dependencies, plain SVG, colors from tokens. Big Pips animate; small ones, like avatars and the route marker, are still images from the same data.

    Room to grow

    The part sheet imports straight into Figma or Rive, and the skeleton maps one to one to Spine, so an animator could take over in a tool later without redrawing anything.

    What I need from you

    1. Does this Pip match your concept? Note anything off in shape, color or proportion.
    2. Which poses and moves feel right, and which feel stiff or wrong? Comment right on the pose.
    3. Should Pip stay teal, or keep taking the line color of the language?
    4. Anything missing from the pose library?
